## Dawn Patrol: Beating Bangalore's Morning Gridlock

To truly experience motorcycling out of Bangalore, you have to leave while the city is still asleep. 

At 4:30 AM, when the outer ring road is cold, empty, and slick with morning mist, the **Royal Enfield Meteor 350** settled into its relaxed cadence. Rolling up the elevated Bellary Road (NH44) toward Bengaluru International Airport, the chilly dawn air cuts through the mesh panels of my Rynox jacket. This is the classic weekend pilgrimage for Karnataka riders, but today was a twin-destination expedition combining the mountain sunrise at **Nandi Hills** with the serene granite expanse of **Adiyogi in Chikkaballapura**.

---

## Part 1: Ascending into the Cloud Sea at Nandi Hills

Leaving the four-lane highway at the Devanahalli toll gate, the road narrows into a quiet, tree-lined avenue leading to the base of the hill fortress. Nandi Hills (ancient *Nandidurga*) rises 1,478 meters above sea level, standing like an island above the surrounding plains.

### The Hill Climb
The approach to the summit features roughly 40 winding curves and sweeping switchbacks. With the tarmac still cool from the night, the Meteor’s low-end torque made the steep gradients effortless. In 2nd and 3rd gear, the bike climbed smoothly without needing high revs, the thump echoing off the stone fortress walls built during Tipu Sultan’s reign.

As I broke through the final switchback near the top gate, the spectacle unfolded: **a sea of pure white clouds rolling across the valley floor**, glowing orange and pink as the first rays of the sun pierced the eastern horizon.

I parked the Meteor along the stone lookout wall, stepped off, and leaned against the granite barrier. The valley below was completely submerged beneath fog, giving the feeling of standing at the edge of the world. Other early-morning riders were gathered in quiet awe, steaming cups of ginger chai warming cold palms as the sun painted the sky in brilliant hues of gold.

---

## Part 2: Rural Byways to Chikkaballapura

By 7:30 AM, as the morning mist began to burn off, I geared back up and began the descent. Descending Nandi Hills in 2nd-gear engine braking, the route bypassed the busy highway traffic by cutting across rural backroads toward **Chikkaballapura**.

These single-lane country roads are where motorcycle touring feels most raw and rewarding:
- Passing lush vineyards, mulberry farms, and sunflower fields.
- Cruising alongside the dramatic granite monoliths of the Eastern Ghats.
- Waving to village school kids and farmers walking alongside the eucalyptus-lined tarmac.

The Meteor was right at home here at 60–70 km/h, absorbing road ruts with its plush rear suspension and steady chassis balance.

---

## Part 3: In the Presence of Adiyogi

Emerging from the rural foothills near Avalagurki, the sheer scale of the destination stops you in your tracks: **the 112-foot bust of Adiyogi Shiva**, set against the dramatic backdrop of the green hills of Chikkaballapura.

The Sadhguru Sannidhi ashram complex is vast and peaceful in the morning. Parking the bike in the designated rider lot, I walked up the stone pathways toward the massive steel sculpture. The sheer geometry and craftsmanship of the face, with the crescent moon and the black Nandi bull facing the shrine, radiates a profound, grounding stillness.

Sitting in the shadow of the monument after hours in the saddle offers the kind of mental reset that only a two-wheeled journey can deliver. No rushing, no deadlines—just the deep hum of the wind across the plains and the presence of ancient stone.

---

## The Return Loop

By mid-morning, I strapped the helmet back on for the return blast south on NH44. The heat was picking up, but with a full morning of mountain curves and spiritual tranquility in the bank, the remaining kilometers back into Bangalore felt effortless.

A 146-kilometer circuit that captures the two distinct souls of Karnataka: the dizzying high-altitude sunrise of Nandi Hills, and the monumental, contemplative stillness of Adiyogi.
