## The Detour: Why Ride Straight When 70 Bends Await?

When I rolled out of Bangalore in the early morning darkness on my **Royal Enfield Meteor 350**, the master mission was set on the GPS: **Rameshwaram** — the southern ocean tip of Tamil Nadu, the historic Pamban Sea Bridge, and the ghost town of Dhanushkodi.

The standard route down NH44 via Hosur, Krishnagiri, and Salem is fast, smooth, and predictable. You lock your throttle, settle into 5th gear, and watch the kilometers tick away. But as Namakkal approached on the trip meter, a legendary wall of green rose sharply against the eastern horizon: **Kolli Malai (The Mountain of Death)** in the Eastern Ghats.

To regular tourists, Kolli Hills is a quiet hill station known for spice plantations and Agaya Gangai waterfalls. But in India’s motorcycling folklore, it is revered for a single intoxicating reason: **70 consecutively numbered, continuous hairpin bends** carved into a sheer mountain cliffside.

Most riders take an entire day to climb it, book a room at the top, and nurse their sore shoulders. My plan was far more intense: **take the detour off the highway, charge up all 70 hairpin bends to the summit at Solakkadu, turn the motorcycle right around at the top, carve all 70 hairpin bends back down to the plains in one go, and immediately rejoin the southern corridor toward Rameshwaram.**

140 hairpin turns in a single morning assault. No overnight stay. No long breaks. Just pure, unadulterated motorcycle geometry.

---

## Part 1: The Ascent — Hairpin 1 to 70 into the Mist

Leaving the sweltering plains of Kalappanaickenpatti, the tarmac narrows as you cross the forest checkpost at Karavalli. Almost immediately, the vertical theatre begins. The very first green-and-white milestone marks: **Bend 1 / 70**.

### Finding the 2nd-Gear Rhythm
The opening 15 bends wind through scrub forest and dry foothills with relatively wide runoffs. But past Bend 20, the mountain closes in. The road turns into a narrow ribbon of asphalt clinging precariously to the rock face, with a granite wall on one side and an abyss on the other.

The Meteor 350 is a cruiser with a relaxed rake and forward footpegs — not an agile supermoto on paper. But its superpower on hill climbs is the **349cc J-series SOHC engine’s bottom-end grunt**. With 27 Nm of torque peaking low at 4,000 RPM, the bike pulled like a tractor:
- Brake hard before corner entry, trail-braking lightly with the rear pedal.
- Drop down into **2nd gear**.
- Turn your head completely toward the exit, look through the corner, countersteer, and lean.
- Roll on the throttle as the bike stands up, letting that thumping single-cylinder torque catapult 191 kg of machine and saddlebags up the steep incline.

### Facing the Green Titans (TNSTC Buses)
Around Bend 35, the real obstacle appeared: **Tamil Nadu State Transport (TNSTC) buses**. These drivers treat the 70 hairpin bends like their personal racetrack. On blind corners, they take up the entire width of both lanes on the apex.

At Bend 42 and again at Bend 58, I had to brake to a crawl right against the outer concrete guard stones, feathering the clutch at 10 km/h with an empty void below my right boot to let a roaring bus scrape past before gunning the throttle again.

By Bend 62, the stifling heat of the plains was gone. The air turned crisp and damp, smelling of crushed eucalyptus and mountain pine. Clouds drifted across the tarmac.

Then came the final milestone: **Bend 70 / 70**. I rolled into the highland hub of Solakkadu at 1,300 meters above sea level.

## Part 2: The Summit Turnaround — No Rest on the Ridge

I pulled over at the Solakkadu junction. 

The Meteor’s air-oil-cooled engine was gently ticking as heat radiated off the blacked-out cylinder fins. The sides of both tyres were scrubbed clean to the edges. Local stalls were steaming with hot ginger-cardamom tea, and weekend drivers were stepping out of their hatchbacks to stretch.

I took off my helmet, drank half a bottle of water, checked tyre pressures by hand, and inspected the luggage straps. 

*“Room booked at the resort, sir?”* asked a local shopkeeper handing me a fresh banana.
*“No anna,”* I said with a grin. *“Turning around right now. Going straight back down to the base and riding to Rameshwaram tonight.”*

He paused with a confused smile and shook his head: *“Up and down in one ride? Saami... your arms will fall off.”*

I strapped the helmet back on, clipped the chin-strap, thumbed the rotary starter, and swung the handlebar around.

---

## Part 3: The Descent — Gravity, Front-End Feel & 70 Bends Down

If climbing 70 hairpins is a test of low-end torque and throttle timing, **descending 70 hairpins is a masterclass in front-end discipline, nerve, and thermal brake management.**

On the way down, the physics inverted completely:
1. **Engine Braking over Pure Brakes**: Riding the brakes continuously down 70 steep switchbacks would boil the DOT 4 fluid and cause severe brake fade before Bend 30. I locked the gearbox into **2nd gear**, letting engine compression hold the downhill momentum to a controllable 25–30 km/h.
2. **The Dreaded Left-Handers**: Going downhill, the tightest corners were the **left-hand hairpins**, where you are hugging the mountain ditch while cranked over at steep downhill pitch angles. Miss the turn-in point by two inches, and you run wide into oncoming traffic.
3. **Weight Transfer**: Every braking zone shifted the entire bike and rider weight onto the front 41mm telescopic forks. The 300mm ByBre front rotor did the heavy lifting, while I used a whisper of rear brake purely to stabilize the chassis.

Bend by bend, the altitude unraveled. Bend 50... Bend 35... Bend 18... The cool pine air gave way to the thick tropical heat of the plains. By the time I crossed Bend 1 and passed the Karavalli gate, I had completed **140 consecutive hairpin bends in under four hours.**

My forearms were buzzing, my clutch fingers felt like coiled springs, and my tyres were radiating heat. But the machine had performed flawlessly — not a single sputter, false neutral, or brake fade.

---

## The Road to Rameshwaram Continues

Rolling back onto the flat tarmac toward Namakkal, I opened the throttle. The Meteor 350 surged into 5th gear at 90 km/h on NH44, enjoying the cool rush of highway wind over the hot engine.

The mountain twisties were conquered; now began the long south-bound haul:
- Cruising past the river plains of Karur and Dindigul.
- Bypassing the historic temple city of Madurai.
- Transitioning onto the coastal stretch of NH49 (now NH87), where crosswinds from the Bay of Bengal began sweeping across the asphalt.
- Crossing the majestic **Pamban Sea Bridge** with open sea on both sides, reaching Rameshwaram Island before nightfall.

Kolli Hills was never on the straightest route to Rameshwaram — but doing **all 70 bends up and down in one shot** made it the defining highlight of the entire southern journey. 140 hairpins in the bank, and the ocean ahead.
